Secure communication between smart meters (SMs) and service providers (SPs) remains a fundamental challenge in smart grid (SG) infrastructures. Because metering data is continuously exchanged over insecure channels, mutual authentication and key agreement (AKA) protocols must ensure confidentiality, anonymity, and resistance to both physical and cyber threats, while remaining lightweight for constrained devices. This article introduces PSUL-SG, an AKA protocol that combines Physical Unclonable Functions (PUFs), the Esch256 hash function, and a reverse fuzzy extractor (RFE). To reduce the computational burden on the SM side, error correction and key reconstruction operations are delegated to the SP. Each SM is provisioned with 1000 stable challenge-response pairs (CRPs) selected at enrollment, stored in a compact format suitable for embedded platforms. The protocol is analyzed under the Random Oracle Model, and its security is verified with the AVISPA tool. Experimental results on the SM side show that PSUL-SG achieves a computation time of 0.355 ms, a communication cost of 960 bits, and an energy consumption of 0.0117 mJ under our evaluation setting. Compared with representative recent schemes under the same cost model, PSUL-SG provides improved efficiency while maintaining strong security guarantees, making it well-suited for large-scale deployment in SG environments.
